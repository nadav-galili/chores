# The world is calm and natural, one green means "act", and the pet is a bird

Three visual decisions that read as arbitrary taste without the context that produced them, recorded so they are not "fixed" later by someone who assumes they were never decided at all. A brand is cheap to choose and expensive to reverse, and the app's whole measurement — does a 7-year-old and a 10-year-old still open it unprompted in week three — is taken on the surface these three decide.

**Calm and natural, because the obvious direction is the one that fails the older child.** A soft off-white ground, a deep forest green, a sage that belongs to the illustrations, and a gold reserved for coins. The rejected alternative is bright-and-arcade — saturated primaries, cartoon bevels, confetti — which is not merely a different taste: it is the look shared by all eight competitors screened in `docs/spec/05-store-listing.md`, and it is the look a 10-year-old reads as "made for babies". She is half the week-3 test, so a direction that alienates her cannot be chosen however well it plays at seven — the same instinct that already keeps "Kids" out of the brand. Clean-and-modern was the other candidate and loses for a structural reason rather than an aesthetic one: with `pet_enabled` off it leaves an empty screen, and that cohort's screen is a real cohort's screen.

**One green means "act", and the growth green never becomes it.** `#2F6B4F` is the action colour and only the action colour; the Grove's greens live inside illustrations, and UI chrome never introduces a second green. The reason is not visual hygiene. The Grove is append-only and is never clawed back (ADR-0011), while coins and the streak both are — a rejection takes back the coins and breaks the streak but never removes a tree. Giving growth and action the same hue would visually equate two mechanics the data model deliberately separates, and would teach a child that the thing which cannot be taken away and the thing which can are the same kind of thing. A "Done" button must not look like a tree. Coin gold is reserved on the same principle, one step further: no button, chip, badge or chrome element uses it, so that colour on screen means coins and nothing else.

**The pet is a small round garden bird, and it starts as an egg.** The species is a five-stage ladder — egg, hatchling, fledgling, full, splendid — chosen because it is the only common creature whose first stage reads as promise rather than as emptiness. A level-1 anything else is a small version of the thing; a level-1 egg is a thing that has not happened yet, and hatching is the strongest early moment in the product available for free, and it lands in the first days, where a competitor loses the child. A plain garden bird rather than a showy one, because the moods are carried by overlays composited onto five bodies and a showy silhouette does not survive being drawn eight times in one style.

None of the three constrains product behaviour. They constrain the palette tokens, the art the app bundles and the copy voice, all of which live in `apps/mobile`; nothing here reaches `packages/shared`, the schema, or `/sync`. Dark mode is out: `userInterfaceStyle` is `"light"` until the art has a dark ground treatment, rather than promising a theme no screen implements. The concrete values these decisions produce — palette, type scale, motion, haptics, the `little`/`big` deltas and the empty-state policy — are recorded in `docs/spec/06-design.md`; this ADR is only why they are what they are.
