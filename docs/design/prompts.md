# Art prompts

Every asset the app bundles, and the exact prompt that produced it. Why the world looks this way: ADR-0012. The concrete values: `docs/spec/06-design.md`. Vocabulary: `CONTEXT.md`.

The art is generated by hand, by the developer, from the prompts below. The failure mode of generated art is not a bad single render — it is style drift between renders, twenty-one images that each look fine and do not look like each other. So the prompts carry the style, the palette and the framing explicitly rather than by reference to a preamble, they are long enough to be boring, and the pet's five stages and the tree's eight forms are written as **successive edits of one creature and one tree**, not as independent prompts.

## How to use this document

1. Work in the order below: pet first, then moods, then trees, then ground, then identity. `pet-l1` and `tree-s1` are the parents of everything after them.
2. For an entry marked **edit of**, do not start a new generation. Load the named image in the model's edit-in-place mode and apply only the change described. If the model loses the creature, re-edit from the parent rather than from the sibling.
3. Paste the prompt **verbatim**. The hex values and the words "soft gouache botanical" are load-bearing; a shortened prompt is a different prompt.
4. Record the seed and date in the entry's table the moment the render is accepted. A re-render in six months is only reproducible if that row is true.
5. Export per **Format and budget** below. Nothing generated is committed by the ticket that writes this document — the seams run on placeholders until the swap ticket.

## The style block

Every prompt below already contains this text inline. It is repeated here only so a drift can be diagnosed against a single reference.

> Soft gouache botanical illustration. Hand-painted opaque gouache on cold-press paper, visible soft brush strokes, gentle paper grain, matte finish, no gloss and no digital gradients. Muted natural palette. Flat background of solid `#F2F6F1` with no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto the background. Soft diffuse light from the upper left, a single quiet contact shadow directly under the subject in a desaturated grey-green. Centred, full subject in frame, generous even margin on all four sides. Square canvas. No text, no letters, no numbers, no watermark, no signature, no border, no frame, no drop shadow, no outline stroke, no cartoon bevel, no glossy highlight, no confetti, no sparkle, no 3D render, no vector look.

Palette the illustrations may use: ground `#F2F6F1`, sage `#8CBF9E`, forest `#2F6B4F`, warm gold `#E9B949` (fruit only), deep green-black `#1B2A22`, and the muted browns and creams the subjects need. Nothing saturated, nothing neon.

**What the style block actually produces, and why that is left alone.** Pass 1 showed the models read this as fine naturalist botanical watercolour rather than as heavy opaque gouache: soft blended washes, almost no visible brush stroke, barely any paper grain. That is not what the words ask for, and it is better than what the words ask for — it is calm, it holds detail at 40 points, and it is dead consistent across all twenty-one. So the block stays exactly as written. Do not "fix" it toward heavier gouache: the wording is what produced the house style, whatever it says on its face.

Step 2 of **Export and post-process** is where that happens, and pass 1 is why.

`android-icon-monochrome.png` is not on this list and is not generated: it is a flat silhouette derived from `android-icon-foreground`, which is what Android's themed-icon slot wants and what a painterly render cannot be.

The pet's fifteen level-and-mood combinations are eight assets, not fifteen: five bodies with three overlays composited on top, which is the shape `apps/mobile/src/lib/pet-art.ts` already has. Keeping the moods as overlays is also what keeps them consistent — one happy overlay is one painting to hold in style, five happy birds are five chances to drift.

## Pet

Five bodies, one creature. A small round garden bird — a robin or a wren, not a peacock, not a parrot, not a bird of paradise. A showy silhouette does not survive being painted eight times in one style, and the moods are carried by overlays rather than by plumage.

The stage names are ADR-0012's: egg, hatchling, fledgling, full, splendid. (`pet-art.ts` currently labels level 3 "Chick" against a placeholder emoji; the swap ticket reconciles that label with these names.)

The bird faces three-quarters to the viewer's left in every stage. The eye is a simple dark round dot with a single small light catch — never a drawn pupil, never eyelashes, never a human expression. Warm and calm, not cute-aggressive.

Each one is a small element in the **upper right quadrant** of an otherwise empty square, on a transparent background, at the same 512×512 canvas as the bodies so it registers without any offset maths. Nothing in an overlay may cover the bird's face.

Sleepy is the one to get right: the pet is sleepy when the child has done nothing today, and it must read as resting, never as sad, disappointed or scolding. No frown, no tears, no downturned brow.

## Tree

Eight forms, **one broadleaf**. The same species, the same trunk character, the same leaf shape, painted eight times at eight ages — not eight trees. A child who reaches form 8 has been finishing days for two months and will have seen every form in between; if form 5 is a different tree from form 4, the ladder stops reading as growth.

Each form must be a **distinct silhouette at a glance**, small on a phone, in a row beside its siblings. The distinction lives in the outline — height, crown width, branch count — not in detail a 40-point-wide tree cannot show.

Blossom arrives at form 6, fruit at form 7, heavy fruit at form 8. Nothing before form 6 carries either. The grove stage each form starts at lives in `packages/shared/src/growth.ts`; the art does not restate it.

Every tree stands on the same shallow patch of pale sage-green ground, its base on the same horizontal line at the same height in every canvas, so eight trees of different heights line up on one ground line in the grove.

**Do not trust the prompt for this.** Pass 1 asked eight prompts to hold one ground line and got two: forms 1–4 landed with the ground patch bottom at 85% of the canvas height, forms 5–7 at 91% and form 8 at 93%. A 6–8% drift is invisible in a single render and obvious in a row of eight. Pixel alignment is not a thing a language prompt controls, so it is a post-process step instead — see **Export and post-process** below, which trims and re-pads every tree onto one line. Keep the wording anyway: it gets the renders close enough that the correction is a shift and never a crop.

### 17. `ground`

The shared ground line the grove's trees stand on: a wide strip tiled horizontally behind a scrolling row of trees. It carries no season, no weather and no time of day — that would multiply the render count fourfold and introduce a date rule that would have to be kept honest against chore date.

## Export and post-process

The models return large square PNGs — Nano Banana returned 1254×1254 for every square asset in pass 1 — and neither the canvas size, the file format, the filename nor the pixel alignment is what the app wants. None of that is worth another prompt attempt. Five deterministic steps turn a folder of renders into the bundle, and pass 1 proved that two of them are not optional.

1. **Rename to the manifest.** The generation tool's filename is not the target filename. `mimbo-icon.png` is `icon`; leading spaces and copy suffixes go.
2. **Drop the painted background on the seventeen in-app assets.** The pet bodies and tree forms come back with `#F2F6F1` painted in, and pass 1 measured it landing anywhere from `rgb(240,244,240)` to `rgb(243,246,242)` — within three of the token and not equal to each other. Against the app's exact `ground` token each one is a faint visible tile, and every asset a slightly different one. Key the flat background out to transparent and let the token show through. This also frees the same art to sit on the parent theme's `#F5F7F5`.
3. **Put the eight trees on one ground line.** Trim each tree to its content, then pad it back to a square canvas with the bottom of the ground patch at exactly 90% of the canvas height and the trunk centred horizontally. Verify by trimming all eight again and confirming one identical bottom edge. The pet bodies already align — pass 1 measured all five within one pixel — but run the same check on them.
4. **Resize to the manifest canvas and convert.** Down to 512, 768, 1536×512 or 1024 as the manifest says, then WebP lossy at quality 82–88 for the seventeen in-app assets, alpha preserved. Downscaling from 1254 is fine; never upscale.
5. **Check the two masked assets before believing them.** Composite `android-icon-foreground` over `android-icon-background`, mask it to a centred circle of 61% of the canvas, and look at the result. If a beak, a tail or a leaf tip is cut, scale the foreground down and repeat. Do the same with a squircle. This is the check that pass 1 failed.

`android-icon-monochrome.png` is produced here too, not generated: take the finished foreground, flatten it to a single flat silhouette, and keep it inside the same 60% box.

## Format and budget

**Single 3× WebP per in-app asset.** One file, no `@1x`/`@2x` set — no shipping device is 1×, and a three-file set triples the thing that has to stay in style. The canvases in the manifest are already the 3× pixel sizes; export lossy at quality 82–88, with the alpha channel preserved on all seventeen. Do not trace to SVG: it destroys a painterly illustration. Do not host remotely: bundled art is what keeps the offline guarantee honest.

**The four identity assets are the exception and stay PNG.** Expo's icon, adaptive-icon and splash slots take PNG, so `icon`, `android-icon-foreground`, `android-icon-background` and `splash-icon` ship as 1024×1024 PNGs, and they are the reason the budget below is not smaller.

Everything is referenced through a static require map. Metro cannot resolve a dynamic asset path, so no filename is built at runtime anywhere.

## The validation pass

Twenty-one renders is too many to discover a bad prompt at the end of. Before generating the rest, render **four** assets, look at them together at phone scale, and correct the prompts from what they show. A prompt fault caught here costs four assets instead of twenty-one.

**The four:** `pet-l1`, `pet-l4`, `tree-s1`, `tree-s6`.

They span the two subjects and both ends of both ladders. `pet-l1` and `tree-s1` are the two parent generations everything else is edited from, so a style fault in either propagates to nine assets. `pet-l4` is three edits deep and is where the edit chain either holds the creature or loses it. `tree-s6` is the first form carrying blossom, which is the single most likely place for a muted palette to go bright.

**What to check** — all four side by side, then again at 40–60 points wide:

1. Do they read as one hand? Same stroke weight, same paper grain, same edge quality.
2. Is the background the same off-white in all four, and is it actually `#F2F6F1` when sampled?
3. Is the light from the upper left in all four, with the same shadow softness and the same shadow colour?
4. Is the palette still muted across all four — nothing saturated, nothing neon, gold nowhere but fruit?
5. Is `pet-l4` recognisably the creature `pet-l1`'s egg would hatch into — plain and round, not showy?
6. Are `tree-s1` and `tree-s6` the same species, and do their bases sit at the same ground line height?
7. At phone scale, is each subject still legible, or has the detail turned to mud?
8. Has anything crept in that the prompt forbade — text, a frame, a drop shadow, a vignette, an outline?

**Record the pass here.** One row per rendered asset, then the corrections made to the prompts above and the reason for each. If a correction changes a prompt that has already been used, the affected asset is re-rendered, not kept.
